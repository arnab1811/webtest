The Brief

All five destinations are within 50 minutes of Wageningen, require no reservations (except Hoge Veluwe), suit a packed lunch perfectly, and offer the kind of quiet that makes a summer afternoon genuinely restorative. River banks, wild floodplains, forested ridges, open heathland — this corner of Gelderland and its borders is quietly exceptional.

01
🌾
Ginkelse Heide & Het Wijde Veld
Open heathland on the Veluwe's edge — closest, calmest, completely free
🚗
Drive from Wageningen ~20 min · 18 km
🅿️
Parking Free · Multiple lay-bys
🎟️
Entry Free
⏱️
Ideal Duration 1.5 – 2.5 hrs
What to Enjoy
  • Vast open heathland with forest fringes — genuine Veluwe character without the entry fee
  • Picnic tables and wooden benches scattered along the paved cycle path
  • Remarkably quiet on weekday mornings; a good breeze almost always blows across the heath
  • WWII historical significance — this was a drop zone for Allied paratroopers in September 1944; interpretive signs throughout
  • In August–September: purple heather bloom transforms the entire landscape
Getting There & Parking

Take the N781 from Wageningen toward Ede. Follow signs for Ginkelse Heide. There are several informal parking lay-bys along the Ginkelseweg and Wiekseweg — all free, no time limits. The area is open 24 hours.


What to Do
  • Walk the perimeter path (approx. 4 km loop) for the full heathland panorama
  • Lay a blanket in the open field — there are sheltered grassy hollows near the forest edge
  • Bring binoculars: lapwing, skylark, and buzzards are common overhead
💎 Hidden Gem

Walk 10 minutes east toward the tree-line and you reach the Quadenoord forest — a sudden canopy of ancient beech and oak completely invisible from the main heath. On a hot afternoon, the temperature drops noticeably the moment you step under the trees. Almost nobody goes this far. Bring a book.

02
🦌
De Hoge Veluwe National Park
The iconic Veluwe — sand dunes, forest, free white bikes, and Van Gogh
🚗
Drive from Wageningen ~40 min · 35 km
🅿️
Parking ~€4 at entrance
🎟️
Entry (adult) ~€14–15
⏱️
Ideal Duration 3 – 5 hrs
What to Enjoy
  • Over 5,500 hectares of forest, drifting sand dunes, heathland, and moorland lakes — Europe's largest privately-owned nature reserve
  • Free white bicycles included with entry — no booking, no deposit, just take one
  • Red deer, wild boar, and mouflon sheep roam freely — early morning sightings are common
  • The Kröller-Müller Museum inside the park holds the world's second-largest Van Gogh collection (separate ticket)
  • The Jachthuis Sint Hubertus — a stunning 1920s hunting lodge — visible from the bike path
Getting There & Parking

Use the Hoenderloo entrance (Houtkampweg 13) for the northern approach — less congested than Otterlo or Schaarsbergen. Book tickets online at hogeveluwe.nl to avoid queuing. Parking is charged separately at the gate.


Perfect Picnic Spot
  • Cycle ~3 km from the Hoenderloo gate to the Koperen Kop viewpoint — open heath, wind, and a 360° horizon
  • Or follow the sand dune path to the Pampelse Zand — an inland desert landscape for a surreal Dutch lunch setting
💎 Hidden Gem

Most visitors hug the main cycling routes. Take the unmarked footpath behind the Museonder (underground nature museum) and walk due north for 20 minutes into the Deelense Veld — a remote moorland valley where you will almost certainly be alone. The sound is just wind and birds. A genuinely rare experience for the Netherlands.

03
🌊
Waalbanddijk Picknickplaats, Ochten
Sit on the Waal river bank and watch Europe drift by
🚗
Drive from Wageningen ~20 min · 17 km
🅿️
Parking Free · Roadside on dijk
🎟️
Entry Free
⏱️
Ideal Duration 1 – 2 hrs
What to Enjoy
  • Direct, unobstructed views across the wide Waal — the main arm of the Rhine through the Netherlands
  • Steady procession of Rhine barges, container ships, and occasional passenger vessels — mesmerising with a coffee
  • Dedicated picnic area with benches and tables right on the dijk crest
  • Absolute calm: the dijk path is popular with local cyclists but rarely crowded
  • Willow-lined floodplain below the dijk — walk down to the water's edge on the river side
Getting There & Parking

Cross the Waal via the A15 bridge at Tiel, then head west along the Waalbandijk toward Ochten. The picnic area is signed from the dijk road. Park directly on the dijk verge — no restrictions. Note: during high-water events (not a summer concern) the access road may flood.


While You're There
  • Walk 10 minutes west along the dijk crest for a broader panorama
  • A small snack stand operates seasonally a short walk toward Ochten village
  • Excellent sunset spot if you time your return for early evening
💎 Hidden Gem

Descend from the dijk on the landward side and walk into the Neder-Betuwe polder — flat, impossibly green, entirely silent except for cattle and a distant tractor. It is one of the most archetypically Dutch landscapes in the entire Gelderse Vallei, and virtually no tourists ever see it because there is nothing to do there, which is exactly the point.

04
🐎
Millingerwaard, Kekerdom
The wildest floodplain in the Netherlands — horses, beavers, and a secret tea garden
🚗
Drive from Wageningen ~50 min · 48 km
🅿️
Parking €3/day · Church, Kekerdom
🎟️
Entry Free
⏱️
Ideal Duration 2.5 – 4 hrs
What to Enjoy
  • Rewilded Rhine floodplain with free-roaming Konik horses and Galloway cattle — keep 25 m distance
  • Oxbow lakes, willow carr, gravel banks, and flood channels — a genuinely wild landscape
  • Beavers active at dawn and dusk; look for the gnawed stumps near water channels
  • Extraordinary birdlife: kingfisher, great egret, white-tailed eagle occasionally overhead
  • No cars allowed inside — the silence is striking
Getting There & Parking

Drive via Nijmegen, cross the Waal, and head northeast toward Kekerdom. Park at the village church (Kerkstraat) — €3 for the whole day, honesty box. From the church it's a 5-minute walk to the reserve entrance. Alternatively, free parking is available further along the approach road but requires a longer walk.


While You're There
  • Walk the 11 km Trage Tocht loop — varied, well-signed, spectacular
  • Bring sturdy footwear — paths are narrow and can be muddy near channels
  • The Theetuin (tea garden) is approx. halfway round — a café in the middle of the reserve, utterly unexpected
💎 Hidden Gem

Follow the inner path north of the main loop toward the Erlecomse Plas — a large oxbow lake that most walkers miss. On a still summer morning the water is a perfect mirror of the sky and willows. If you are very quiet and arrive early, kingfishers work this stretch reliably. It is, by some margin, one of the most beautiful inland spots within an hour of Wageningen.

05
🌲
Boswachterij Amerongse Berg
The Netherlands' highest ridge — cool, forested, and blissfully quiet
🚗
Drive from Wageningen ~35 min · 28 km
🅿️
Parking Free · Landal car park
🎟️
Entry Free
⏱️
Ideal Duration 1.5 – 3 hrs
What to Enjoy
  • Dense beech and pine forest on the Utrechtse Heuvelrug ridge — the highest natural point in this part of the Netherlands (69 m)
  • Cool under canopy even on hot summer days — genuinely refreshing
  • Separate signed trails for walkers, cyclists, horse riders, and mountain bikers — no conflict, good peace
  • Authentic forest silence: remarkably quiet even on sunny weekends
  • Nearby Amerongen village has a beautiful 17th-century castle (Slot Amerongen) and a historic tabakschuur (tobacco barn) run by enthusiastic volunteers
Getting There & Parking

Drive west from Wageningen via the N225 through Rhenen toward Amerongen. Follow signs for Amerongse Berg / Landal. The main car park at the Landal resort entrance is free and has good trail access. From the village of Amerongen itself, the trail to the summit is a pleasant 20-minute walk through mixed woodland.


While You're There
  • Walk the summit loop (approx. 5 km) for panoramic views over the Rhine valley and Betuwe
  • The tabakschuur volunteers are excellent and will tell you the full colonial history of Dutch tobacco — a conversation worth having
  • Combine with a coffee stop in Amerongen village (Café de Grebbe is good)
💎 Hidden Gem

From the ridge summit, take the unmarked track descending northward toward the Grebbelinie — the old Dutch Water Line defence fortifications. You pass through a landscape that is simultaneously ancient (17th-century earthworks) and lush green. The view of the Gelderse Vallei from the earthworks on a clear day is one of the finest inland panoramas in the central Netherlands, and almost completely unknown to visitors from outside the region.

Before You Go — Practical Tips

🧺

Pack Smart

Carry your lunch in a proper insulated bag — summer temperatures can hit 28–32°C. A thin blanket doubles as a picnic mat on grassy spots. Bring more water than you think you need.

🦟

Insects

Ticks are active in the Veluwe and Millingerwaard from April onwards. Wear long trousers near heathland and do a tick check after returning to the car. Mosquito repellent is useful near river floodplains at dusk.

🌤️

Timing

Aim to leave Wageningen by 9–10 AM to get the best of the morning light and avoid the midday heat on open heathland. River spots and forested areas are pleasant all day.

📱

Navigation

Google Maps works well for all five. For Millingerwaard, navigate to Kerkstraat, Kekerdom. For Amerongse Berg, navigate to Landal Amerongse Berg, Amerongen. Download offline maps for areas with patchy signal.

🐕

Dogs

Ginkelse Heide requires dogs on a leash. Hoge Veluwe does not permit dogs at all. Millingerwaard requires a leash due to free-roaming livestock. Amerongse Berg has a designated off-leash zone — check signs at entry.

🌸

Best Weeks

For heather bloom on Ginkelse Heide and Hoge Veluwe: mid-August to mid-September. For wildflowers in Millingerwaard: June–July. Amerongse Berg and Waalbanddijk are excellent throughout the summer season.
