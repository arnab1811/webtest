💳 Payments & Money

How Payments Work in Norway

Norway is one of the world's most cashless countries. Cards are accepted virtually everywhere.

💳
Card is King
Visa and Mastercard debit/credit cards are accepted everywhere — supermarkets, apartments, restaurants, transport. You will rarely need cash. Contactless tap-to-pay is universal.
🏨
Apartment Payments (Booking.com)
Most apartments use Pay at Property — your card is held as guarantee only, no charge until check-in/out. Some private apartments charge upfront via Booking.com. Always check "Payment Policy" before confirming.
💱
Always Pay in NOK
When the card terminal asks "Pay in NOK or your home currency?", always choose NOK. Choosing EUR or another currency activates Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC), adding a 3–5% fee by the merchant's bank.
🏦
Notify Your Bank
Before departure, notify your bank that you'll be using your card in Norway. Some banks block foreign transactions without prior notice. Bring at least two cards (Visa + Mastercard, or debit + credit) as backup.
📱
Fee-Free Travel Cards
Consider using Revolut, Wise, or Monzo as your primary card to avoid foreign transaction fees (typically 1.5–3% per transaction). This alone can save €30–50 over a 9-day trip for 3 people.
💵
Do You Need Cash?
Rarely. Cards work everywhere including bus taps, small shops, and markets. Keep ~€45 (≈500 NOK) cash as an emergency backup. ATMs (called Minibank) are widely available in all three cities.
🔒
Damage Deposits
Enter Tromsø — Exclusive Apartments requires a NOK 5,000 (~€435) damage deposit held on arrival by credit card. Ensure your card has sufficient headroom. Released after check-out if no damage.
🍺
Alcohol Cutoffs
Beer/cider (max 4.7%) in supermarkets: until 20:00 Mon–Fri, 18:00 Sat, not at all on Sunday. Wine and spirits only from Vinmonopolet (state shop), closed Sundays and 18:00 on weekdays.
💱
Exchange Rate (June 2026): 1 EUR ≈ 11.5 NOK · 1 USD ≈ 10.6 NOK · 1 GBP ≈ 13.5 NOK. Norway's VAT on food is 15%, on other goods 25% — all prices in shops are inclusive of VAT, nothing hidden.

💡 Top Money-Saving Tips for Norway

🏷️
Buy "First Price" Brand
Norway's budget own-label (equivalent of AH Basic). Available at KIWI and REMA 1000 on pasta, rice, oil, dairy. Saves 20–35% vs branded products.
🐟
Eat Norwegian Salmon & Lamb
Fresh salmon is caught locally — excellent quality and price. Lamb is cheaper than in the Netherlands. Lean into these for dinners rather than beef or imported items.
💧
Drink the Tap Water
Norwegian tap water is among the world's best. Carry a refillable bottle instead of buying bottled water — saves €3–5 per day for 3 people.
⏰
Shop Early at Bakeries
Bakeries like Godt Brød offer early-bird discounts before 10am. Going first thing gets cheaper pastries and coffee — ideal before day-trip explorations.
🛒
Ready Meals from EUROSPAR / Coop
Hot food counters at EUROSPAR and Coop Extra offer cooked meals at €5–9 per person — far cheaper than any restaurant, good for tired evenings.
📅
Plan Sundays in Advance
Shop on Saturday afternoon for Sunday provisions. Stock up on bread, eggs, cheese, fruit, and beverages. Never rely on Sunday opening — most stores are closed by law.
📊 Overall Budget Reality Check: For 3 people over 9 days in Norway (2 nights Bergen + 3 nights Ålesund + 3 nights Tromsø), budget approximately €1,400–1,800 on accommodation (self-catering apartments) and €800–1,100 on food (majority self-catered, a few meals out). This is roughly €750–900 per person total for accommodation + food — before flights, activities, and transport.
